# MedAI — Round 2 Product Requirements Document
## AI-Native Local Care Intelligence Engine

Version: 1.0
Status: Product Definition
Platform: MedAI Clinical Intelligence System

---

## 1. Product Overview

MedAI's Round 2 capability extends its existing clinical intelligence system with an AI-native Local Care Intelligence Engine.

The existing MedAI platform transforms fragmented medical documents into structured clinical history, identifies potential safety concerns, detects longitudinal inconsistencies, tracks clinical trends, and provides evidence-grounded answers.

The new engine addresses the next step:

When MedAI identifies a clinically relevant concern that may require professional review, the system helps the user identify an appropriate type of healthcare professional and discover real healthcare providers available near the user's location.

The engine is designed as a distinct intelligence layer rather than a simple doctor-search feature.

Its responsibility is to transform:

Clinical evidence
→ clinical concern
→ appropriate care category
→ appropriate specialty
→ user context
→ real local providers
→ explainable provider recommendations

The system must not diagnose disease or replace professional medical judgment.

---

# 2. Problem Statement

Medical records frequently contain fragmented information originating from different providers, facilities, and time periods.

Even when an intelligent system successfully identifies a potential concern, the user may still face an important practical question:

"Who should I consult about this?"

A generic healthcare search is insufficient because the appropriate provider depends on the nature of the detected concern.

Examples:

- A cardiovascular-related concern may require cardiovascular specialist review.
- A medication interaction may require review by the prescribing clinician, physician, or pharmacist.
- A persistent laboratory abnormality may require an appropriate physician or specialist depending on the clinical context.
- An uncertain or low-confidence finding may require general medical review before specialist escalation.

Therefore, MedAI requires an intelligence layer that connects clinical findings with appropriate real-world care options.

---

# 3. Product Goal

Build an AI-native care-navigation engine that:

1. Understands structured clinical concerns generated by MedAI.
2. Determines an appropriate healthcare specialty or care category.
3. Considers confidence, risk level, evidence, and clinical context.
4. Collects the user's location and consultation availability.
5. Retrieves real healthcare providers from publicly available sources.
6. Filters and ranks providers according to relevance and user context.
7. Explains why the recommended provider type is relevant.
8. Clearly identifies the source of provider information.
9. Handles missing or unavailable provider data safely.
10. Never invents healthcare providers or presents synthetic information as real.

---

# 4. Product Principles

## 4.1 Clinical Safety First

MedAI identifies possible concerns; it does not diagnose.

All recommendations must maintain an appropriate clinical disclaimer.

The system must never communicate:

"You have disease X."

Instead:

"Your records contain findings that may warrant clinical review."

---

## 4.2 Evidence Grounding

Care recommendations must originate from the clinical evidence already identified by MedAI.

The Round 2 engine should not independently invent patient history.

---

## 4.3 AI-Native Reasoning

The engine should not be implemented as a large collection of hardcoded mappings such as:

"blood pressure → cardiologist"

Instead, AI should interpret the structured clinical concern and determine the most appropriate care category using the available evidence and context.

Deterministic software should be used for operations that should not depend on LLM interpretation, such as:

- API validation
- geographic distance calculations
- provider filtering
- data normalization
- duplicate removal
- required-field validation
- safety constraints

---

## 4.4 Real-World Provider Data

Every provider presented as a real healthcare option must originate from a real publicly available data source.

Synthetic or fabricated provider information is prohibited.

If the provider source does not provide a field such as rating or phone number, the UI must clearly indicate that the information is unavailable rather than generating it.

---

## 4.5 Explainability

The system should explain:

- What concern was detected.
- What evidence supports the concern.
- What type of healthcare professional is suggested.
- Why that care category is relevant.
- Why the displayed providers are relevant.

---

# 5. Target Users

Primary user:

A person or healthcare professional using MedAI to review a patient's medical history and determine an appropriate next-care option.

The system may support different user roles already available within the existing MedAI authentication architecture.

# 7. Functional Requirements

## FR-01: Clinical Finding Input

The engine must accept structured findings generated by the existing MedAI clinical intelligence layer.

The input should support information such as:

- Patient identifier
- Clinical concern
- Risk level
- Confidence level
- Evidence
- Relevant diagnoses
- Symptoms
- Medications
- Laboratory findings
- Vital trends
- Conflicting instructions
- Document references
- Temporal information

The engine must use the existing structured information rather than requiring the user to manually re-enter the clinical issue.

---

# 8. FR-02: Clinical Concern Interpretation

The Concern Interpreter analyzes the detected issue and creates a normalized care-navigation representation.

Example:

```json
{
  "concern": "Repeated elevated blood pressure readings",
  "riskLevel": "moderate",
  "confidence": 0.91,
  "evidence": [
    "Repeated elevated blood pressure measurements across visits"
  ],
  "careContext": "persistent_abnormal_vital"
}
```

The interpretation must remain grounded in the available patient evidence.

---

# 9. FR-03: Specialty Reasoning

The Specialty Reasoner determines the most appropriate healthcare specialty or care category.

The reasoning must consider:

- Clinical concern
- Evidence
- Risk level
- Confidence
- Relevant medications
- Relevant laboratory findings
- Relevant symptoms
- Longitudinal context

The engine should support appropriate levels of specificity.

For example:

```text
Clinical concern
       ↓
General medical review
       OR
Specific specialist
       OR
Medication-focused review
```

The engine should avoid unnecessary specialist escalation when the available evidence does not justify it.

---

# 10. FR-04: Uncertainty Handling

The engine must explicitly handle uncertain cases.

If the clinical evidence is insufficient to confidently identify a specialty, the system should recommend an appropriate general medical consultation rather than making an overly specific recommendation.

Example:

> "The available records suggest that professional medical review may be appropriate. The records are not sufficient to confidently identify a specific specialist."

The system should never manufacture certainty.

---

# 11. FR-05: User Location

The system must request the user's location when provider discovery is initiated.

The user should be able to provide:

- City
- Area
- Locality
- Other recognizable location information supported by the provider search service

The system should not require exact coordinates from the user unless the underlying provider API requires them.

---

# 12. FR-06: Consultation Availability

The system must ask the user about their consultation availability.

Examples:

- This week
- Today
- Weekdays
- Evenings
- Weekends
- Flexible

Availability should be treated as a user preference unless the provider source exposes actual appointment availability.

The system must not claim that a provider is available at a specific time unless the underlying data source verifies it.

---

# 13. FR-07: Provider Discovery

The engine must retrieve real healthcare providers from a publicly available provider/location data source.

Possible sources include:

- Google Maps / Google Places
- OpenStreetMap
- Nominatim
- Other legitimate public healthcare directories

The final implementation must select a reliable source appropriate for the deployment environment.

Provider retrieval should support:

- Name
- Specialty/category
- Address
- Location
- Distance where available/calculable
- Rating where available
- Contact number where available
- Source/reference information

---

# 14. FR-08: Provider Data Validation

Before displaying a provider, the system must validate that the provider originated from the configured external source.

The system must:

- Reject malformed provider records.
- Remove duplicate results.
- Avoid fabricated fields.
- Preserve unavailable fields as unavailable.
- Prevent the AI model from generating provider records.

The LLM must never be responsible for inventing provider data.

---

# 15. FR-09: Provider Relevance Ranking

Retrieved providers should be ranked according to multiple signals.

Potential ranking factors include:

1. Clinical specialty relevance
2. Provider/category match
3. Geographic proximity
4. User location
5. User consultation preferences
6. Provider data completeness
7. Rating where available
8. Other legitimate provider metadata

The ranking architecture should separate:

AI-derived clinical relevance

from

deterministic provider attributes.

Conceptually:

```text
Clinical relevance
        +
Specialty match
        +
Location relevance
        +
User preferences
        +
Provider quality signals
        ↓
Provider relevance score
```

The system must not fabricate ranking inputs.

---

# 16. FR-10: Recommendation Explanation

Each recommendation should provide a concise explanation.

Example:

> "MedAI identified repeated elevated blood-pressure readings across multiple visits. Based on the available clinical evidence, a physician specializing in internal medicine may be appropriate for further evaluation."

The explanation must distinguish between:

- Evidence observed in the records
- AI interpretation
- External provider information

---

# 17. FR-11: Provider Result Presentation

The UI must display a clear list of real provider options.

Each result should show, where available:

- Provider/clinic name
- Specialty/category
- Address
- Distance
- Rating
- Contact number
- External source or map action

Example:

```text
Provider Name
Internal Medicine / General Physician

Address
Distance

Rating: 4.6
Phone: +XX XXXXXXXX

Why recommended:
Relevant specialty for the clinical concern.

[View Provider]
[Get Directions]
```

---

# 18. FR-12: No-Result Handling

If no suitable providers are found, the system must not generate alternatives from imagination.

It should clearly communicate:

> "No suitable providers were found in the selected area."

It should then allow the user to:

- Widen the search area.
- Change location.
- Review another care category where appropriate.

---

# 19. FR-13: API Failure Handling

External provider services may fail, timeout, or return incomplete information.

The system must handle:

- Timeout
- Rate limit
- Invalid response
- Empty response
- Network failure
- Missing fields
- Provider service unavailability

The UI must communicate the problem clearly without exposing internal technical details unnecessarily.

---

# 20. FR-14: Safety Boundary

The system must clearly communicate that:

- MedAI does not provide a medical diagnosis.
- The detected issue is based on available uploaded records.
- Provider recommendations are intended to help locate appropriate professional care.
- A qualified healthcare professional must make the final clinical decision.

High-risk findings should receive stronger safety messaging.

---

# 21. AI Responsibility Model

The architecture should clearly separate AI reasoning from deterministic application logic.

### AI Responsibilities

- Interpret clinical findings.
- Summarize the clinical concern.
- Determine an appropriate care category.
- Reason about specialty relevance.
- Handle ambiguous clinical contexts.
- Generate explanation grounded in provided evidence.

### Application Responsibilities

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Patient isolation
- Data validation
- Location processing
- Provider API requests
- Geographic calculations
- Provider deduplication
- Provider filtering
- Ranking calculations
- API error handling
- Source preservation
- Safety enforcement

### External Provider API Responsibilities

- Real-world provider data
- Address
- Location
- Rating where supported
- Contact information where supported
- Provider/category metadata

This separation is fundamental to system reliability.

---

# 22. Non-Functional Requirements

## NFR-01: Security

Existing MedAI authentication and patient-level data isolation must remain enforced.

Round 2 APIs must never allow one authenticated user to access another user's patient information.

---

## NFR-02: Reliability

A failure in the external provider API must not crash the MedAI backend.

AI failures must return controlled application responses.

---

## NFR-03: Explainability

Recommendations must have a traceable reasoning basis.

Clinical evidence should remain accessible through the existing MedAI evidence/source mechanisms.

---

## NFR-04: Performance

The system should provide a responsive user experience.

Provider retrieval and AI reasoning should be performed asynchronously where appropriate, with visible loading states.

---

## NFR-05: Maintainability

The Round 2 engine must be implemented as a modular service layer rather than embedded directly inside frontend components or existing Round 1 services.

---

## NFR-06: Provider Data Integrity

No provider information may be synthesized.

All provider-facing information must originate from an external provider source or deterministic transformation of that source.

---

# 23. Suggested Service Architecture

The implementation should follow the existing MedAI modular architecture.

Conceptually:

```text
backend/src/services/care/

    concern.service.js
    specialty.service.js
    provider.service.js
    ranking.service.js
    recommendation.service.js
```

The exact filenames may be adapted to the existing codebase.

### concern.service

Transforms Round 1 findings into a normalized care concern.

### specialty.service

Uses AI reasoning to determine appropriate care categories.

### provider.service

Communicates with the external provider/location API.

### ranking.service

Ranks retrieved providers using validated clinical and geographic signals.

### recommendation.service

Coordinates the complete Round 2 workflow and produces the final structured response.

# 24. Suggested Structured Output

The Care Intelligence Engine should return structured data rather than unstructured LLM text.

Example:

```json
{
  "concern": {
    "summary": "Repeated elevated blood pressure readings",
    "riskLevel": "moderate",
    "confidence": 0.91
  },
  "careRecommendation": {
    "category": "general_medical_review",
    "specialty": "Internal Medicine",
    "reason": "Repeated abnormal blood pressure measurements require professional clinical assessment."
  },
  "userContext": {
    "location": "Jaffna",
    "availability": "Evenings"
  },
  "providers": [],
  "safety": {
    "isDiagnosis": false,
    "requiresProfessionalReview": true
  }
}
```

Provider objects must contain only validated external provider information.

# 28. Out of Scope

The following capabilities are intentionally excluded:

- Medical diagnosis
- Autonomous treatment decisions
- Prescription generation
- Medication dosage recommendations
- Emergency medical dispatch
- Autonomous appointment booking unless explicitly supported by the provider API
- Fabricated provider information
- Synthetic ratings or reviews
- Autonomous multi-agent planning
- General-purpose medical chatbot functionality
- Replacing a physician's clinical judgment

---

# 29. Design Philosophy

The Round 2 Care Intelligence Engine should be understood as a bridge between clinical intelligence and real-world healthcare access.

Round 1 answers:

> "What does the patient's medical history indicate?"

Round 2 answers:

> "Given that finding, what type of professional care may be appropriate, and what real local options can the user explore?"

The engine must remain:

- Evidence-grounded
- AI-native
- Explainable
- Location-aware
- Provider-data-driven
- Safety-conscious
- Modular
- Extensible

---
